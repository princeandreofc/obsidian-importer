The thesis

Love is not a claim.
It is a connection you can shape.

What you are holding is your catalogue turned into one continuous dive: your site, your film, your records and your stages, rebuilt as an interactive cinema. Every section of alilove.world is there, exactly as it stands today — and each one now lives inside a world of its own, breathing with the music.

It was not generated. It was written — line by line, as code. The planets are mathematics. The rose is geometry with a seed. The grooves of the vinyl are drawn by a function. That is the point of this whole proposal:

The machines never replaced the artists. In electronic music they became the instruments — the 808 did not end drummers, it birthed house. This project is written inside that same tradition: programming as art, by a human hand.

The pages that follow show the lineage this work belongs to, the piece itself, the protections built into its source code, and the campaign it unlocks — including pre-save, wired in the honest way.

The lineage

A century of visual music

  • 1921Silent cinema — visual music is born. Oskar Fischinger paints music onto film, frame by frame, by hand.
  • 1958John Whitney. The computer becomes a poetic instrument — motion is composed, not decorated.
  • 1966Liquid light shows. Oil, glass and human hands, performed live above the dancefloor.
  • 1969Nam June Paik. The electronic image becomes something you play, like a synth.
  • 1974Kraftwerk. One total identity across sound, object, stage and image.
  • 1980Roland 808 / 707. The drum machine replaces no one — Chicago and Detroit turn it into liberation.
  • 1990Rave flyers & demoscene. Cheap print and home computers: code becomes community art.
  • 2001Processing & creative coding. The artwork itself becomes executable — and performable.
  • 2026THE SPIRAL. Your catalogue as a living, programmed world — the next verse of this song.

In electronic music, the visual identity and the programming were never two crafts. This lineage is the argument.

The piece · II

Three new instruments

The Resonance Rose. The campaign's living emblem: twelve petals, six crystal axes, a memory spiral, two counter-rotating orbital fields and a warm core in cool light — generated from a seed, slightly asymmetric, so it feels alive rather than corporate. It greets you in the sky of the opening, returns at Listen, and closes the finale.

Resonance Session. A thirty-second piece about connection: isolated particles tremble; aligned groups form bridges; the room remembers the final shape. Tune, shape, capture. No score, no game over, no account — the capture is the trophy.

VJ Studio 2.0. A live instrument: five scene banks — Orbit, Groove, Garden, Ritual, Day Signal — four macros — Energy, Depth, Memory, Connection — one-key scene changes, recording straight from the stage. Built to travel from the browser to TouchDesigner and Resolume for festival-scale shows.

The principle

Made by hand.
Protected by design.

Nothing here was scraped or taken. The engine is handwritten generative code: every planet, petal, groove and world is a function, not somebody's photograph. From the great covers of this culture we inherit grammar — night, repetition, the single monumental object — never pixels.

The music is locked by default. This is real code from the source, not a promise in a slide:

const podeTocar = m => !!m
  && m.status === "cleared-production"
  && m.webPlayback === true
  && m.interactiveSync === true
  && m.visualizerUse === true;   // no rights, no sound

If a track's rights are not documented, the stage stays silent. The release system is embargo-safe the same way: it refuses to invent titles or dates. Recording captures visuals only, unless a track is explicitly cleared for capture. The ethics are compiled into the artwork.

Where AI served: as an instrument in human hands — sketching, unblocking, accelerating code that a human artist designed, directed, reviewed and signed. The same relationship a producer has with a drum machine. Humans make the art. The tools keep the tempo.
